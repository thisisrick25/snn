# Abstract

**Version: Challenge → Insight → Contribution**

Catastrophic forgetting remains a fundamental barrier to deploying neural networks in real-world continual learning scenarios, where systems must acquire new knowledge while preserving previously learned skills. While artificial neural networks (ANNs) rapidly lose acquired knowledge upon training on new tasks, biological brains exhibit remarkable continual learning capabilities alongside sparse neural activity patterns. Spiking neural networks (SNNs), which naturally manifest sparse, event-driven communication, present a promising substrate for continual learning; however, the relationship between spike sparsity and catastrophic forgetting remains unexplored. In this work, we investigate how varying levels of spike sparsity affect continual learning performance in SNNs. We systematically control sparsity through three mechanisms: spike threshold adjustment, winner-take-all inhibition, and activity regularization, evaluating their impact on task retention, accuracy, and energy efficiency across sequential learning paradigms. Our findings reveal that moderately sparse SNNs exhibit significantly reduced forgetting compared to both dense SNNs and ANN baselines, with an identifiable optimal sparsity region that balances learning capacity and interference minimization. These results suggest that leveraging sparsity as a design principle offers a biologically grounded and computationally efficient pathway toward mitigating catastrophic forgetting in neuromorphic systems.
